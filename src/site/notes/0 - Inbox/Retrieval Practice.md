**Retrieval practice** (the act of recalling facts from memory) strengthens the memory and interrupts forgetting.
- A single, simple quiz after reading a text or hearing a lecture produces better learning and remembering than rereading the text or reviewing lecture notes.
- while the brain is not a muscle that gets stronger with exercise, the neural pathways that make up a body of learning do get stronger, when the memory is retrieved and memory is practiced.

# The Cap on Retrieval
There's a lower cap on retrieval than there is on learning. Not every memory you have stored is readily available for you to retrieve. If that were the case, it would be really hard to sort through all your memories when thinking and making decisions.

Part of that lower cap on retrieval is that the ability to recall knowledge is determined by the context, recent use, and the number of vivid cues linked to that piece of knowledge. And often times, these cues need to be updated in order to match the current status quo. (e.g. updating cues from a previously learned language to support the learning of a new language). 

However, when mastery is achieved, it's safe to forget because that knowledge is easily relearned. The knowledge itself is not forgotten, it's the cues that help retrieve it that have been forgotten. 

# Diminishing Returns
The easier  the knowledge or skill is for you to retrieve, the less your retrieval practice benefits the retention of it.
