Reflection can involve several cognitive activities that lead to stronger learning: 
- retrieving knowledge and earlier training from memory (think [[0 - Inbox/Retrieval Practice\|Retrieval Practice]])
- connecting these to new experiences (think [[1 - Projects/Reading Making It Stick/Elaboration\|Elaboration]])
- visualizing and mentally rehearsing what you might do differently next time (think [[1 - Projects/Reading Making It Stick/Elaboration\|Elaboration]])

Then there comes a time when you get into a tight situation and it's no longer a matter of thinking through the steps, it's a matter of reflexively taking the correct action.

Unless you keep recalling this maneuver, it will not become a reflex. (Think about things in your job or your life that you do. The things that need to become a reflex, or second nature, or whatever, need to be drilled as described in the paragraph above)

- Reflection - the act of taking a few minutes to review what has been learned from an experience and asking yourself questions.
	- Some good reflection questions to ask after learning a new concept (or attending a lecture): 
		- what are the key ideas?
		- what are some examples?
		- how do these relate to what I already know?
	- Some good reflection questions to ask after an experience
		- what went well?
		- what could have gone better?
		- what might I need to learn for better mastery?
		- what strategies might I use the next time to get better results?
	- Reflection can involve [[0 - Inbox/Retrieval Practice\|Retrieval Practice]], [[1 - Projects/Reading Making It Stick/Elaboration\|Elaboration]], and [[1 - Projects/Reading Making It Stick/Generation effect\|Generation effect]]
